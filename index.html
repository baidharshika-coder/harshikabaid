osllo · 2024–2026

why does childhood have an age limit?

in january 2024, we acquired my baby store, one of bengaluru's largest kids' retail businesses. i ran it for the next two years, and in early 2026 we rebranded it to osllo.

300k+families trusted us
3stores run
150+brands managed
50+events curated

why osllo

celebrate childhood, don't correct it.

most kids' brands start with what's wrong. a checklist of problems and their fixes: boredom, learning gaps, screen-time battles, busy schedules. we took a pause and chose to build a world that celebrates childhood instead.

then we asked a bigger question. why does childhood have an age limit? it isn't an age. it's a feeling. that's how osllo was born: a world where childhood belongs to all ages.

the rebrand

my baby store stopped fitting.

if childhood isn't limited to an age, the brand couldn't be either. so we reimagined everything: the name, the philosophy, the feel of the space, and the world around it.

the stores

150+ brands. 5,000+ skus.

products across four categories: play, wear, care and go. safe, special and surprising finds, thoughtfully curated for babies, toddlers, preteens and the grown-ups who love them.

experiences

when parents stopped coming in, we gave them a reason.

sales dipped across kids' retail, and parents weren't stepping out of their homes. so we started parent–kid experiences in the store, a safe space for kids and parents to actually express themselves. it grew to 50+ events.

learning to run it

i didn't even know what a kpi was.

when i started, i couldn't have told you what a good margin looked like, or why stock sitting on a shelf was costing us money.

two years later, i was running every part of the business. inventory: what to buy, what was ageing, and how fast it needed to move before it trapped our cash. finance: cash flow, and what our margins should actually be. the floor: how many of the people who walked in left with something, and how much they spent. and around all of it, the staff, the suppliers and the stores themselves.

kinder to the planet

small changes, all of them real.

brands like ariro, superbottoms and baby forest on the shelves. bamboo clothing, reusable cloth diapers and wooden toys. price tags redesigned as colour-in doodle cards, so something disposable became a keepsake. vendor packaging reused as gift boxes.
